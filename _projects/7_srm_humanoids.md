I joined **SRM Team Humanoid** as an undergraduate and led it in my final year. The team was new and under-resourced — in my first year we had six Dynamixel actuators, not enough to build a single biped. Harder than the missing hardware was the missing guidance: nobody at the university worked on humanoid robotics, so I learned what I needed from MOOCs, textbooks, and papers. I spent months trying to make a biped walk and watched it stumble, fall, and crash instead.

As team lead I spearheaded the development of three 20-DoF humanoid robots:

- **Shadow and Ghost** — the twins
- **Charlie** — a fully 3D-printed 20-DoF interactive humanoid with an onboard chatbot

### Results

- **RoboGames 2017**, San Francisco — I led the team to **India's first gold medal**, in the autonomous humanoid penalty kick event, along with **Silver** (humanoid freestyle), **Silver** (biped race), and **Bronze** (RC humanoid sumo).
- **RoboGames 2015**, San Francisco — Bronze medal.
- **Third place**, Humanoid Application Challenge (Robot Magic), IROS 2017, Vancouver.
- My graduation project on humanoid teleoperation was named **Best Graduation Thesis** in the department and was accepted for poster presentation at IROS.

Most importantly, by the end I had a walking algorithm that actually worked robustly — which mattered more to me than any of the medals.

### Related work

- **Real-Time Teleoperation of a Humanoid Robot with Motion Imitation and Legged Locomotion** — *ICARM 2018*
- **Dynamic Lateral Balance of Humanoid Robots on Unstable Surfaces** — *ICEECCOT 2017*
- **Dynamic Whole-Body Imitation of Human Motions in Humanoid Robot** — *IROS 2017 (poster)*

Other projects from this period included a humanoid that played tic-tac-toe, a humanoid that autonomously rose after a fall, a gesture-controlled robotic arm using an Xbox Kinect for depth processing and inverse kinematics, and a serial-manipulator plotter arm.
